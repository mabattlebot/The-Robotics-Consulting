How to Build a VEX IQ Drivetrain

Step-by-step guide for building a fast, stable, and competition-ready VEX IQ drivetrain.

Home › Articles › Drivetrain Guide

Building an efficient drivetrain is one of the most important steps in creating a competitive VEX IQ robot. A good drivetrain affects speed, maneuverability, stability, and overall performance.

1. Understanding Drivetrain Types

  • Tank Drive: Each side of the robot is controlled independently. Simple, stable, and easy to program.
  • Mecanum or Omni Wheels: Allows the robot to move sideways (holonomic drive). Requires precise alignment and more programming skill.
  • H-Drive: Uses a combination of wheels for omnidirectional movement. Compact but more complex to build.

2. Selecting Wheels and Motors

  • Choose wheels that fit your robot size and game field conditions.
  • Match motors to your drivetrain design. More motors = more torque, but also more weight.
  • Consider gear ratios carefully for speed vs. torque.

3. Building Tips

  • Keep your drivetrain symmetrical to ensure straight movement.
  • Use structural pieces to strengthen the frame and prevent wobbling.
  • Test your drivetrain frequently during construction to catch misalignments early.
  • Plan space for other mechanisms like intakes or lifts to avoid interference.

4. Programming and Testing

  • Test your drivetrain in driver control mode first to get a feel for responsiveness.
  • Adjust motor speeds and direction as needed for smooth turns.
  • In autonomous, carefully program distances and turns using sensors to improve accuracy.

With the right design, careful testing, and incremental improvements, your VEX IQ drivetrain can become the foundation of a strong, competitive robot.
